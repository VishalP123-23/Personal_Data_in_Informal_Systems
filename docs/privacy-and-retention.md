# Privacy and retention notes

The app uses synthetic demo information only. Its notice and retention policy seeds are examples, not clinic legal advice. Retention entries are marked unverified with no fixed number of days. Usernames, display names, contact details, appointment timestamps, and user-entered notes and messages are encrypted at the application layer with AES-GCM. Keyed HMAC digests support identity lookup without storing searchable plaintext. Passwords continue to use Argon2 password hashing. The key is stored outside the database in the ignored local backend/.env file; protect and back it up separately. This local key-file approach is a demo control, not a production key-management design.

The clinic privacy officer reviews ordinary access, correction, and limited erasure requests. A patient full-data erasure request goes directly to the security-admin queue; no other officer reviews or executes that full-deletion request. The admin sees counts for profile, appointments, consent records, clinical notes, prescriptions, privacy complaints, patient-linked audit events, and older request records; the active erasure case is kept as minimal review evidence. The admin can select categories only after recording a retention assessment and confirming the action. Profile deletion disables the demo account and scrubs its login/contact identifiers. Erasure actions are recorded. This workflow covers only records represented in this app: backups, external systems, application logs, and unmodeled records require separate handling; records subject to a verified retention obligation may be kept. Retention policies in the demo are unverified, so this is not a legal determination or complete deletion guarantee. PostgreSQL remains the source of truth; after commit, the generated appointments.json mirror refreshes automatically. The JSON file contains an encrypted appointment snapshot and a visible record count, not plaintext appointment fields.

Consent history currently demonstrates only an optional appointment-reminder purpose. It does not implement every possible lawful basis, does not send reminders, and does not replace a full privacy notice or clinic-specific legal review.

Audit metadata excludes passwords, session secrets, and full clinical text. Backups, export files, local disk recovery, and physical access require separate retention and deletion controls before real use.

CCTV footage is not ingested by this demo. If added, identifiable footage should be treated as personal data, including incidental footage of staff, visitors, and bystanders. Define and notify a specific purpose, keep only footage needed for it, encrypt stored clips, restrict and log access, and document a reviewable deletion schedule. The retention period must be set against the clinic’s actual purpose and any applicable legal duties; the DPDP Rules do not give this demo a blanket CCTV retention period. Store clips in a restricted encrypted media store, with only case references and retention metadata in PostgreSQL.
